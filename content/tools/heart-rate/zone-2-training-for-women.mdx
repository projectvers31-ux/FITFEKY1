Zone 2 is the most under-prescribed, most over-felt, and most under-trained zone in women's fitness. It's the kind of cardio that feels too easy to be working — and that's exactly the point.

This guide explains what Zone 2 is, why it matters more for women than for men, and how to use the [FitFeky heart rate zone calculator](/tools/heart-rate) to get your exact numbers.

## What is Zone 2?

Heart rate training splits your effort into five zones, based on a percentage of your maximum heart rate:

- **Zone 1** (50–60%): Very light, warm-up
- **Zone 2** (60–70%): Fat-burning, aerobic base
- **Zone 3** (70–80%): Aerobic, "tempo"
- **Zone 4** (80–90%): Threshold, hard
- **Zone 5** (90–100%): Maximum, intervals

Zone 2 is the level you can hold a conversation at. You can feel your heart working, but you're never gasping. Sweating starts around 15–20 minutes in. It feels, frankly, boring — and that's why most people skip it.

## Why Zone 2 is the secret to fat loss

At Zone 2, your body is preferentially burning fat for fuel — roughly 50–60% of calories come from fat, versus 30–40% at higher intensities. You can sustain the effort for 60–120 minutes, so total fat oxidised is much higher than a 20-minute HIIT session.

A landmark 2021 review in *Sports Medicine* found that Zone 2 training improves:

- Mitochondrial density (more "engines" in your cells)
- Capillary density (better blood flow to muscles)
- Fat oxidation rates
- Insulin sensitivity
- Resting heart rate over time

These are the foundations that make every other workout — including HIIT and lifting — work better.

## Why it matters more for women

Three reasons:

1. **Hormonal compatibility.** Zone 2 doesn't spike cortisol the way HIIT does. For women, whose cortisol response to intense training is already higher, this is a quieter, more sustainable stress.
2. **Menstrual cycle support.** Most women tolerate Zone 2 work in any cycle phase. Higher-intensity work is best cycled with the luteal and follicular phases.
3. **Recovery from strength training.** Zone 2 is active recovery that builds your aerobic engine without digging into the recovery budget you need for lifting.

In short: Zone 2 is the cardio that *complements* women's bodies, not fights them.

## The 80/20 rule for women

The most-cited endurance training principle is the **80/20 rule**: 80% of your weekly cardio minutes should be in Zone 2 or easier. The remaining 20% can be in Zones 3–5.

For a woman doing 4 cardio sessions per week:

- **3 sessions in Zone 2** (45–60 minutes each)
- **1 session of higher intensity** (20–30 minutes, intervals or threshold)

This is the split that built every elite female endurance athlete. It also works for fat loss — better, in many cases, than all-HIIT plans.

## How to find your real Zone 2

The default Zone 2 calculation is **60–70% of max heart rate**, where max HR is estimated as 220 minus your age. But the 220-minus-age formula has a standard deviation of ±10–12 bpm, so the resulting "Zone 2" can be off by 20 bpm.

The **Karvonen method** is more accurate. It uses your resting heart rate, which reflects your actual fitness:

> **Target HR = ((Max HR − Resting HR) × % intensity) + Resting HR**

To use it, you need:

- **Max HR**: from a recent hard effort, or 220 − age as a starting estimate
- **Resting HR**: measured first thing in the morning for 3 days, averaged

## How to do Zone 2 well

- **Walk briskly, cycle, swim, or row.** Anything you can sustain for 45+ minutes while still talking.
- **Hold the line.** The hardest part of Zone 2 is staying *in* Zone 2. If you can't speak a full sentence without gasping, slow down.
- **Track your pace over time.** As your fitness improves, you'll be able to go faster at the same heart rate. That's the adaptation — and the win.
- **Be patient.** Zone 2 results take 6–10 weeks to show up on the scale or in performance, but they're the most durable.

## A simple 4-week Zone 2 plan

| Week | Zone 2 sessions | Time per session |
|------|-----------------|------------------|
| 1    | 2               | 30 min           |
| 2    | 3               | 40 min           |
| 3    | 3               | 50 min           |
| 4    | 3               | 60 min           |

Add one Zone 4 or 5 session per week after week 4. Continue for 8+ weeks before changing anything.

Zone 2 is the most-effective, most-skipped, and most-misunderstood form of cardio for women. It burns fat, builds the aerobic engine, supports hormones, and complements strength training — without the cortisol cost of HIIT.
