If you've ever cut calories, lost 5 pounds, and then watched the scale freeze for three weeks — the problem isn't your discipline. It's the size of your deficit.

The standard "500 calories below maintenance" advice was calibrated for the average man. For women — especially in your 30s and 40s — that same deficit is often too aggressive, and the consequences show up as stalled fat loss, lost muscle, and a missing period.

This guide shows you how to set a deficit that actually works for a female body.

## The science of "too much, too fast"

A 2010 meta-analysis in *Obesity Reviews* found that very low-calorie diets (under 1,200 kcal) cause roughly 25% of weight loss to come from muscle, versus 10–15% on moderate deficits. That's the trade-off: cut more, lose more muscle.

A second consequence is **adaptive thermogenesis** — your metabolism "turns down" in response to under-eating. Research from *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* shows this effect can reach 15% or more on aggressive cuts, meaning a 1,400-calorie target quietly becomes a 1,600-calorie target in real terms.

For women, there's a third cost: **hypothalamic suppression**. Too-large deficits can pause ovulation, disrupt your cycle, and lower thyroid hormone. None of these help fat loss.

## What "moderate" actually means for women

The most-cited sustainable range is a **15–25% deficit below TDEE** (your total daily energy expenditure). Here's how that plays out for a 35-year-old, lightly active woman at 165 cm and 68 kg:

- **TDEE** ≈ 1,950 kcal
- **15% deficit** → ~1,660 kcal
- **25% deficit** → ~1,460 kcal

Notice that 1,500 — the internet's favourite "safe" number for women — is at the *aggressive* end of the spectrum, not the moderate one.

## The 4-step calorie deficit protocol

### 1. Calculate your real TDEE

Use the [FitFeky calorie calculator](/tools/calorie). It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor formula (the most accurate validated equation) and adjusts for your actual activity, not a guess.

### 2. Subtract 250–400, not 500

A smaller deficit feels almost identical day to day but halves your muscle-loss and metabolic-damage risk. For most women, 300 calories is the sweet spot.

### 3. Hit 1.6 g of protein per kg, minimum

Protein is muscle-preserving insurance. A 68 kg woman should eat at least 110 g of protein per day — spread across 3–4 meals for best absorption.

### 4. Lift weights 3× per week

Resistance training is the only reliable signal to your body that you want to keep your muscle. Cardio alone in a deficit shrinks you everywhere — including the parts you wanted to keep.

## The 4 signs your deficit is too big

Watch for these red flags. If you see two or more, add 100–200 calories back for two weeks:

1. **Your sleep is worse than usual** — waking at 3 a.m. or feeling wired at night
2. **Your resting heart rate has dropped 5+ bpm** — a sign of metabolic slowdown
3. **Your menstrual cycle has shortened, lengthened, or skipped** — your hormones are responding to the stress
4. **Your gym performance has stalled or regressed** for 2+ weeks despite consistent training

## How long to stay in a deficit

A sustainable cut for women is 8–12 weeks, followed by 4–6 weeks at maintenance. This isn't a "diet" — it's a series of fat-loss phases bookended by recovery phases. Trying to push longer cuts is how you get stalled, hormonal, and miserable.

## The bottom line

A calorie deficit works. But the size of the deficit determines *what* you lose: fat, muscle, or hormones. For most women, a 15–20% deficit below your real TDEE, with high protein and strength training, is the formula that actually delivers the body — and the results — you want.
