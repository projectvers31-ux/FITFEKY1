If you've ever copied a macro plan from a fitness influencer and felt worse — not better — you weren't crazy. The plan was wrong for your goal, your training, or your hormones.

Macros aren't a single "right answer." They're a tool that needs to be tuned to what you're doing. This guide gives you the four splits we use with women at FitFeky, with the research behind each gram.

## The hierarchy of macros

When you set macros, you're really making four decisions in this order:

1. **Total calories** — the size of the energy bucket
2. **Protein** — the muscle-preserving floor
3. **Fat** — the hormonal floor
4. **Carbs** — everything that's left

The first three are non-negotiable for women. Carbs fill the gap.

## Protein: the muscle-saving floor

Research from *The Journal of Sports Sciences* and *Current Opinion in Clinical Nutrition and Metabolic Care* consistently shows that women need **0.8 to 1.2 g of protein per pound of bodyweight** to preserve or build muscle in any goal.

For a 150 lb woman, that's 120–180 g per day. Higher end if you're lifting heavy, lower end if you're sedentary.

**Why it matters:** Protein is the only macro that actively repairs muscle tissue. Under-eating it is the single most common reason women lose muscle in a cut and stall their fat loss.

## Fat: the hormonal floor

Women's reproductive and stress hormones are built from cholesterol — and cholesterol comes from dietary fat. Cut fat too low and you risk cycle disruption, dry skin, and poor workout recovery.

The research-backed minimum for women is **0.3 g of fat per pound of bodyweight** (or about 20% of total calories, whichever is higher). For most active women, 25–30% is the sweet spot.

## Carbs: the training fuel

Carbs aren't the enemy. They're your muscles' preferred fuel, and the only macro your brain runs on exclusively. Cut them too aggressively and you'll feel flat, foggy, and tired in the gym.

How much you need depends entirely on training volume:

- **Sedentary**: 0.5–1 g per pound
- **Lifting 3×/week**: 1–1.5 g per pound
- **Endurance training**: 1.5–2.5 g per pound
- **Hybrid / CrossFit-style**: 1.5–2 g per pound

## The 4 macro splits we recommend for women

### 1. Fat-loss (cutting)

- **Protein**: 1.0 g per lb
- **Fat**: 0.35–0.4 g per lb
- **Carbs**: fill the rest

For a 150 lb woman on a 1,700 kcal cut: **150 P / 130 C / 55 F**.

The high protein protects muscle. The moderate fat keeps hormones happy. The carbs are enough to fuel 3 lifting sessions a week.

### 2. Maintenance (recomp)

- **Protein**: 0.9 g per lb
- **Fat**: 0.4 g per lb
- **Carbs**: fill the rest

For a 150 lb woman eating 2,100 kcal: **135 P / 165 C / 60 F**.

Recomp is the slowest goal to see on the scale, but the best for body composition. The split is slightly more permissive on carbs because you're not trying to lose weight.

### 3. Lean bulk

- **Protein**: 0.9–1.0 g per lb
- **Fat**: 0.35 g per lb
- **Carbs**: fill the rest, with the surplus going here

For a 150 lb woman eating 2,400 kcal: **140 P / 250 C / 50 F**.

The extra carbs fuel heavy lifting. A small calorie surplus (200–300 kcal above TDEE) builds muscle with minimal fat gain.

### 4. Endurance / high-output

- **Protein**: 0.8 g per lb
- **Fat**: 0.3 g per lb
- **Carbs**: fill the rest, aggressively

For a 130 lb woman training for a marathon at 2,600 kcal: **105 P / 380 C / 40 F**.

This split is for women doing serious endurance work. Carbs dominate because glycogen is the limiting factor for long efforts.

## The 3 mistakes to avoid

### 1. Skipping the protein floor

If your protein is below 0.8 g per lb, you will lose muscle in a deficit. Period.

### 2. Going keto as a woman

Keto works for some women — but it works *worse* for female endurance and strength than it does for men, because of how the female hormonal system relies on glucose. We don't recommend it for most goals.

### 3. Treating macros as permanent

Your macros should change every 8–12 weeks based on your progress, your cycle, and your training. A 6-month-old macro plan is a stale macro plan.
