If you've been weighing yourself daily and watching the number bounce around by 1–2 kg, you're not gaining and losing fat in 24 hours. You're gaining and losing water, glycogen, and gut contents. The scale, by itself, lies.

Body fat percentage is the metric that tells you what's actually changing. Read this guide once and you'll never look at the scale the same way.

## What body fat percentage actually measures

Body composition is the breakdown of your body mass into two categories:

- **Fat mass** — essential fat (vital organs, hormones) plus storage fat
- **Lean mass** — muscle, bone, water, organs, connective tissue

Body fat percentage is fat mass divided by total body weight. A woman weighing 65 kg at 25% body fat is carrying 16.25 kg of fat and 48.75 kg of everything else.

## What "healthy" actually means for women

The American College of Sports Medicine and the American Council on Exercise publish widely-cited ranges:

| Category    | Women 20–40 | Women 41–60 |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Essential   | 10–13%      | 10–13%      |
| Athlete     | 14–20%      | 14–20%      |
| Fitness     | 21–24%      | 21–24%      |
| Average     | 25–31%      | 25–33%      |
| High        | 32%+        | 34%+        |

A few important notes:

- **Under 14% is rarely sustainable for women** and is associated with cycle disruption, bone-density loss, and hormonal issues.
- **The "fitness" range of 21–24%** is what most athletic women who lift weights sit at — and it looks lean, defined, and strong.
- **Age shifts things slightly upward** post-menopause, and that's normal.

## The 4 ways to measure body fat

### 1. The US Navy method (free, instant, ~3–4% accurate)

A validated circumference formula using your neck, waist, and hip measurements. Our [body fat calculator](/tools/body-fat) uses this method. It's free, takes 60 seconds, and is good enough to track trends.

### 2. Smart scales (cheap, ~5–8% accurate)

Bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) scales are improving fast, but the absolute number is noisy. **Use them for trends only** — measure at the same time of day, under the same conditions, and watch the direction, not the number.

### 3. Skinfold calipers (cheap, ~3–5% accurate)

A trainer pinches 3–7 sites and plugs measurements into an equation. Accuracy depends entirely on the operator. A good trainer is great; a bad one is useless.

### 4. DEXA scan (gold standard, expensive)

A full-body X-ray that breaks down fat mass, lean mass, and bone density. The most accurate method available outside a research lab. Costs $50–150 per scan, recommended quarterly, not weekly.

## How to take the US Navy measurement correctly

The biggest source of error in any circumference method is *you*. To get a usable number:

1. **Use a flexible vinyl tape measure.** Cloth or metal tape won't give consistent results.
2. **Measure on bare skin.** Clothing compresses.
3. **Stand relaxed.** Don't suck in, don't flex.
4. **Neck:** Measure just below the larynx (Adam's apple), tape horizontal.
5. **Waist:** Measure at the navel, tape horizontal, relaxed exhale (don't suck in).
6. **Hip:** Measure at the widest point, usually mid-butt.

Take the measurement 3 times and average. The first attempt is almost always the worst.

## What to track instead of the daily scale weight

If you want the body-composition equivalent of "progress photos," track these once a month:

- **Body fat %** (from US Navy or a DEXA scan)
- **Waist circumference** at the navel
- **Hip circumference** at the widest point
- **Strength in 3 lifts**: squat, bench, deadlift (or push-up, plank, glute bridge)
- **Progress photos** in the same lighting, same time of day, same angle

If those four numbers are all moving in the right direction, the scale doesn't matter.

## How often to measure

Daily is too noisy. Weekly is still noisy. **Monthly is the sweet spot.** Hormonal cycles alone cause a 1–2 kg shift week to week, so weekly measurements during the luteal phase (week before your period) will look misleadingly bad.

For research-grade tracking, do a DEXA scan every 3–6 months. For most women, monthly US Navy measurements are plenty.

## How to actually lower body fat

Three things, in order of importance:

1. **A moderate calorie deficit** — 300–500 calories below TDEE. See our [calorie calculator](/tools/calorie) for your number.
2. **High protein** — 1 g per pound of bodyweight minimum, ideally 1.2 g.
3. **Strength training** — 3–4 sessions per week, progressive overload.

The combination of these three — not endless cardio, not aggressive cuts, not fat-burner supplements — is the formula that lowers body fat percentage while keeping every ounce of muscle you have.

## The bottom line

Body fat percentage is the number that actually answers the question you care about: *"Am I getting leaner?"* The scale can't answer that. Circumference measurements, a smart use of the US Navy method, and quarterly DEXA scans will.
