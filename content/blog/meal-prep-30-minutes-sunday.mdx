The Pinterest version of meal prep: 4 hours on Sunday, 15 identical glass containers, chicken and broccoli for lunch until the end of time.

The real-life version: 30 minutes, 3 components, enough variety to not hate your lunch by Wednesday.

## The framework: 1 protein, 1 carb, 1 veggie, repeated differently

You don't need 5 different recipes. You need 1 protein cooked 2 ways, 1 carb cooked 1 way, 1 veggie prepared 2 ways. Mix and match across the week.

### The protein: cook 1.5–2 lbs, season it twice
- 1 lb plain (salt, pepper, garlic powder, olive oil) — uses 1, 2, 3
- 0.5–1 lb with a different spice blend (taco, curry, Italian, lemon-herb) — uses 4, 5

Same pan. Same cooking time. Two seasonings. Done.

### The carb: cook a big batch of one thing
Rice, quinoa, sweet potatoes, or whole-grain pasta. ~2 cups dry = 4–5 servings.

### The veggie: roast a tray
Toss broccoli, peppers, and onions with olive oil and salt. 25 minutes at 425°F. Done.

## The 30-minute timeline

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Preheat oven to 425°F. Get out one sheet pan and one skillet. |
| 0:05 | Chop veggies for the sheet pan. Toss with oil. Into the oven. |
| 0:10 | Start rice or quinoa in a pot. |
| 0:15 | Season 1.5 lb of protein one way, 0.5 lb the other way. |
| 0:20 | Cook the plain protein in the skillet. |
| 0:25 | Cook the seasoned protein in the same skillet. |
| 0:30 | Everything's in the oven or done. Clean up, store in containers. |

**Total active time: 30 minutes.** Total passive time (rice cooking, oven roasting): another 30 minutes. You can do other things while these run.

## The 5-day lunch rotation

| Day | Protein | Carb | Veggie | Sauce |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Plain chicken | Rice | Roasted broccoli | Soy + ginger |
| Tue | Plain chicken | Quinoa | Roasted peppers | Salsa + lime |
| Wed | Seasoned chicken (taco) | Rice | Roasted onions | Hot sauce + avocado |
| Thu | Seasoned chicken (curry) | Sweet potato | Roasted broccoli | Greek yogurt + curry paste |
| Fri | Plain chicken | Pasta | Roasted peppers | Olive oil + parmesan |

Same ingredients, five different meals. No two lunches in a row are identical, but you're not cooking every day.

## The freezer is your friend

Anything you don't eat by Wednesday goes in the freezer. Most cooked proteins and grains freeze fine for 2–3 months. Veggies are best fresh, so under-cook them slightly and add a fresh element when you reheat (spinach, fresh herbs, lemon juice).

## The "I forgot to prep" backup plan

Sometimes Sunday doesn't happen. The backup plan is the **3-component freezer bag**: a bag of cooked rice (orzo, quinoa), a bag of cooked protein, a bag of roasted veggies. All in the freezer. On a busy day, dump them in a hot pan, add a sauce, eat in 6 minutes.

It takes 15 minutes of prep once a month to maintain. It's the lazy person's meal prep — and it works.

## The honest take

Meal prep is a tool, not a religion. The goal is to make the *default* decision a healthy one. If your fridge has cooked chicken and roasted veggies at 12:30 PM, you'll eat them. If it doesn't, you'll order in.

The system above is 30 minutes, costs ~$30–40 for the week, and removes the daily "what am I eating" decision fatigue. That's the win.
