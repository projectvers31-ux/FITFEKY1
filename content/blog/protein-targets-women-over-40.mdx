The Recommended Dietary Allowance for protein — 0.8 g per kg of body weight — is the minimum to avoid deficiency. It is *not* the optimal intake for fat loss, muscle preservation, or healthy aging.

For women over 40, the optimal number is roughly 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day. That's a large gap from 0.8, and it has real consequences.

## Why protein needs to climb with age

### Sarcopenia is real and starts earlier than you think
Muscle mass begins declining at ~1% per year after age 30, accelerating to 1.5–2% per year after 60. The clinical term is sarcopenia, but the pre-clinical version — losing muscle without noticing — starts in the 40s.

### Protein synthesis becomes less efficient
A landmark 2009 study in *PLoS ONE* by Symons et al. found that the muscle protein synthetic response to a given dose of protein is **~30% lower in older adults** than in younger ones. You need more protein to trigger the same muscle-building signal.

### Protein is the most satiating macronutrient
In a calorie deficit, hunger is the enemy of consistency. Protein scores higher than both carbs and fat on the satiety index, and higher protein diets consistently outperform lower protein diets in randomized weight-loss trials — at the same calorie count.

## The numbers, by goal

| Goal | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance / general health | 1.2–1.6 g/kg | Floor for active women |
| Fat loss with muscle preservation | 1.6–2.0 g/kg | The sweet spot for over-40 |
| Active strength training | 1.8–2.2 g/kg | If you're lifting 3+ times/week |
| Over 60 or actively losing muscle | 2.0–2.4 g/kg | Upper end of the evidence |

A 70 kg woman in perimenopause aiming for fat loss with muscle preservation: **112–140 g protein per day.**

That's roughly 30–40 g per meal across 3 meals, plus a snack if needed.

## How to hit it without forcing it

### 1. Front-load breakfast
Most women under-eat protein at breakfast (a piece of toast and a latte is ~5 g) and over-eat it at dinner. Flip that. Aim for 30+ g at breakfast — Greek yogurt, eggs, cottage cheese, protein smoothie, smoked salmon.

### 2. Use the "palm + thumb" serving rule
Each meal: a palm-sized portion of protein (chicken, fish, lean beef, tofu) plus a thumb-sized portion of dense protein (cheese, nuts). This is roughly 25–35 g for most women.

### 3. Protein snacks beat carby snacks
A protein shake, a hard-boiled egg with hummus, Greek yogurt with berries, or cottage cheese with pineapple are all 15–25 g and will keep you full for hours.

### 4. Don't fear late-night protein
A 2021 study in *Cell Reports Medicine* found that pre-sleep protein (30 g of casein) improved muscle recovery without disrupting sleep. A small protein snack before bed is fine and can help you hit your target.

### 5. If you can't hit it from food, supplement
Whey protein isolate is the most-studied, highest-absorbing option. A scoop in water or a smoothie is 25 g of protein for ~120 kcal.

## The leucine threshold

There's one more nuance. The muscle protein synthesis signal kicks off when a single meal contains **~2.5–3 g of leucine**. That's roughly:

- 30 g of whey protein (leucine-rich)
- 35 g of animal protein (chicken, beef, fish, eggs)
- 40–50 g of plant protein (lower leucine content per gram)

If you're plant-based, this matters more — you need larger total protein servings to trigger the same signal.

## The bottom line

The 0.8 g/kg RDA is not a target; it's a deficiency threshold. For women over 40 aiming for fat loss, muscle preservation, or healthy aging, the evidence-supported range is 1.6–2.2 g/kg — distributed across 3–4 meals of 25–40 g each.

This is the single most important dietary change you can make after 40 for body composition.
