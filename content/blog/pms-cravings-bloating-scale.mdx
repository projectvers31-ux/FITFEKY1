It's the night before your period. You're on the couch, halfway through a sleeve of dark chocolate, and the scale says you're up 3 pounds from yesterday.

Breathe. None of that is fat. Let's walk through what's actually happening.

## The 4 (or so) pounds of "gain" before your period

In the luteal phase, three things change that move the scale without changing your body composition:

1. **Water retention from progesterone.** Progesterone causes the kidneys to retain sodium, which holds water. Typical swing: 1–3 kg (2–6 lb) of water.
2. **Glycogen supercompensation.** Your body stores extra carbs as glycogen in the luteal phase, and each gram of glycogen pulls 3–4 g of water with it. This is energy *for* a possible pregnancy — your body doesn't know you're not trying.
3. **Gut contents.** Slower transit time + fiber-rich comfort foods = literally more food in your digestive tract at any given moment.

None of this is fat. The water will come off in 3–5 days after your period starts. The glycogen normalizes with the cycle. The food moves through.

**Rule: never weigh yourself in the 5 days before your period if the number stresses you out.** Or weigh daily, ignore the luteal phase spike, and trust the trend.

## Why the cravings are real (and not weakness)

Two hormones drive luteal-phase cravings:

- **Progesterone** stimulates appetite — it's literally the hormone that prepares your body to eat more to support a potential pregnancy.
- **Serotonin drops** in the late luteal phase. Serotonin is made from tryptophan, and low serotonin drives carbohydrate cravings specifically — *especially* for chocolate, which contains both sugar (raises serotonin) and a small amount of theobromine (a mild stimulant).

A 2017 study in *Appetite* found women consumed an average of **240–500 more calories per day** in the late luteal phase versus the follicular phase. This is biology, not a character flaw.

## What to do (and not do)

### Do
- **Eat 100–200 more kcal in the luteal phase.** You're burning more — match intake to output. A small bump prevents the "I starved all week and ate a pizza Friday night" cycle.
- **Eat the chocolate, sometimes.** A square of dark chocolate won't derail a deficit. Refusing it for 10 days and then eating 1,200 calories of it in one sitting is worse than just having it.
- **Prioritize magnesium and B6.** Both are involved in the luteal → menstrual transition, and small deficiencies amplify cramps and cravings. Food sources: pumpkin seeds, dark leafy greens, bananas.
- **Move gently.** Hard intervals feel terrible on day 1–2 of your period for many women. Walking, yoga, and light strength are fine and often reduce symptoms.

### Don't
- **Don't cut calories drastically to "make up" for the gain.** That 3 pounds is water. Cutting deeper will spike cortisol, which will retain *more* water.
- **Don't weigh yourself in the luteal phase and make decisions based on that number.** Compare this week to the same week last cycle.
- **Don't blame yourself for cravings.** Your hormones are pulling the strings. The job is to respond skillfully, not to feel guilty.

## A weekly pattern that respects your body

| Phase | Energy | Calories | Training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Period (days 1–4) | Lowest | Maintain | Walk, yoga, light strength |
| Follicular (days 5–13) | Rising | Moderate deficit | Push hard, PRs |
| Ovulation (day 14) | Peak | Moderate deficit | HIIT, max lifts |
| Luteal (days 15–28) | Variable | +100–200 kcal | Steady, zone 2, recover |

## The bottom line

Your weight will fluctuate 2–5 pounds every single month. That is normal, hormonal, and *not* fat. If you're tracking the scale, track it across full cycles — not across days.

If your weight is going up *consistently* across 3+ cycles in the same week of your cycle, that's a trend. Otherwise, it's just your body doing its monthly job.

**Stop weighing yourself before your period. Trust the trend. Eat a square of chocolate if you want it.**
