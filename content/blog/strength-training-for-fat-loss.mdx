The default weight-loss prescription is still "eat less, move more, do more cardio." It's not wrong, exactly — but it's incomplete in a way that causes most women to regain the weight.

Cardio burns calories *during* the workout. Strength training changes how many calories your body burns *all day, forever, even at rest*. That's the difference.

## The metabolism math

A 70 kg woman with 30% body fat has ~21 kg of fat mass and ~24 kg of lean mass. The lean mass — mostly muscle — burns roughly 13 kcal/kg/day at rest. Fat burns ~4.5 kcal/kg/day.

The math:
- 24 kg of muscle burns ~310 kcal/day at rest
- 21 kg of fat burns ~95 kcal/day at rest

**Muscle burns ~3.3x more than fat per kilogram.** Add 2 kg of muscle and your resting metabolism is ~25 kcal/day higher. Doesn't sound like much — but it's 9,000 kcal/year, or ~2.6 lb of fat.

But here's the bigger point: strength training doesn't just *add* muscle. It *preserves* the muscle you have during a calorie deficit, which is the difference between losing 70/30 fat-to-muscle versus 90/10.

## What happens without strength training

A 2010 review in *Obesity Reviews* compared weight loss composition across studies:

| Approach | Fat lost | Muscle lost |
|---|---|---|
| Diet only | 75% | 25% |
| Diet + cardio | 80% | 20% |
| Diet + strength training | 95% | 5% |

If you lose 20 pounds on diet alone, 5 pounds of that is muscle. Your metabolism has dropped, and you've made it harder to keep the weight off.

If you lose 20 pounds on diet + strength training, only 1 pound is muscle. You look the same at a lower weight, you feel stronger, and your metabolism has barely budged.

## The 4-movement framework

You don't need a 6-day PPL split to get these results. Four movements, twice a week, cover the bases:

### 1. Squat pattern — goblet squat, dumbbell front squat, or leg press
Trains: quads, glutes, hamstrings, core
Why: largest muscle group in your body; biggest metabolic and hormonal response

### 2. Hip hinge — Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, glute bridge
Trains: glutes, hamstrings, lower back
Why: corrects the anterior tilt that comes from sitting; builds the glutes that drive a higher BMR

### 3. Push — dumbbell press, push-up variation, overhead press
Trains: chest, shoulders, triceps
Why: counterbalances the rounded-shoulder posture; functional strength

### 4. Pull — row, lat pulldown, band pull-apart
Trains: back, biceps, rear delts
Why: every other movement in this list is push; you need balance

That's it. 4 movements, 3 sets of 8–12 reps, twice a week. The rest of your training can be whatever you enjoy.

## How to pair it with cardio

Don't replace cardio. Combine.

- **Lifting first, cardio after** (if you can) — better strength performance, slightly less effective cardio
- **Cardio first, lifting after** (if your gym is busy at peak times) — better cardio performance, slightly compromised lifting
- **Separate them on different days** (best) — full intensity in both

Steady-state cardio (zone 2) is best for fat loss because it's sustainable and doesn't spike cortisol. Save HIIT for once or twice a week at most.

## The honest take

The "lose weight" industry has spent 30 years telling women that cardio and low-calorie diets are the answer. They work for 3 months. They fail for 5 years. The women who keep the weight off — and the National Weight Control Registry is unambiguous about this — are the ones who lift weights and eat moderate amounts of protein.

The shift is small. The compounding is large.
