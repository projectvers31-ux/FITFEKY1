You start a diet. Week 1: 4 pounds gone. Week 2: another 1.5. Week 3: nothing. Week 4: still nothing. You panic. You cut more calories. The scale moves 0.2 pounds. You conclude your metabolism is broken.

This pattern — drop, then stall at the 3-week mark — is so common it's almost universal. It's not a sign that anything is wrong. It's a sign that something very specific is happening, and you can predict it.

## What's actually happening to your weight

The scale measures *body mass*, not *fat mass*. Body mass = fat + muscle + water + glycogen + gut contents. When you start a deficit, several things happen simultaneously:

### Week 1: water drops
Glycogen stores are depleted as you reduce carbs. Each gram of glycogen releases 3–4 g of water. So a 300 g loss of glycogen = 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) of water. This is why the first week is dramatic — it's not 4 pounds of fat, it's mostly water.

### Weeks 2–3: real fat loss begins
You start losing actual adipose tissue at a rate of 0.5–1 lb/week (assuming a moderate deficit). This is slower and less visible on the scale.

### Week 3–4: water bounces back
As cortisol rises from the deficit, sleep worsens slightly, training stress accumulates, and your body retains water as part of the stress response. This masks continued fat loss.

**The math:** You might be losing 1 lb/week of fat the entire time, but the scale shows nothing for 2–3 weeks because water is oscillating.

## The diagnostic: is it really a plateau?

A true plateau is **3+ weeks of zero weight change** with consistent tracking. Less than that, you're not in a plateau — you're in noise.

Ask yourself:
1. **Are you tracking accurately?** A 2017 study in *Obesity* found self-reported calorie intake underestimates by 30–50%.
2. **Are you weighing at the same time, same conditions?** Daily weigh-ins, then average across 7 days, is the gold standard.
3. **Has your training volume or intensity changed?** More stress = more water retention.
4. **Are you in your luteal phase?** Add 1–2 weeks to your timeline.

If you've answered yes to all four and *truly* have 3+ weeks of flat weight, you're in a real plateau. About 1 in 4 dieters hits one.

## The fix: don't cut more, do these instead

### 1. Take a diet break
A 2017 study in *International Journal of Obesity* found that dieters who took 2-week diet breaks at the 3-week mark lost *more* weight at 16 weeks than those who dieted continuously. Why? Reduced cortisol, restored leptin, better adherence.

A diet break = eat at maintenance for 2 weeks, then return to deficit.

### 2. Recalculate your TDEE
You weigh less now. Your TDEE has dropped. Run your numbers again — your deficit from 12 pounds ago is no longer a deficit.

### 3. Add a strength session
If you're only doing cardio, add 2 short strength sessions per week. Muscle is metabolically active, but more importantly: strength training signals your body to *keep* muscle, not cannibalize it.

### 4. Sleep 7+ hours
This is non-negotiable. Sleep deprivation increases ghrelin, decreases leptin, raises cortisol, and tanks insulin sensitivity. A single week of 5-hour nights can wipe out the metabolic benefit of a 500-calorie deficit.

### 5. Walk more
Adding 3,000–5,000 steps per day (on top of your normal activity) is the most underrated fat-loss tool. It's low-stress, doesn't spike cortisol, and burns 150–250 kcal/day.

## When to actually cut calories

If you've done all of the above for 2–3 weeks and the scale is *still* flat, then — and only then — reduce by another 100–150 kcal. But keep protein high. And never go below your BMR for more than a few weeks.

## The bottom line

The 3-week stall is not a plateau. It's water. It's cortisol. It's your body responding to a deficit. The fix is rarely "eat less" — it's "sleep more, train smarter, and wait."
